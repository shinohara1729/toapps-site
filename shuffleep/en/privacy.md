The Operator exports purchase and subscription transaction records from RevenueCat, together with customer IDs and records relating to consent to automatic renewal, to private storage controlled by the Operator (Cloudflare R2) (for retention, see Article 6(4)).

RevenueCat’s customer ID (which RevenueCat calls an “anonymous ID”) is issued without entering a name, but it is an identifier linked to purchase information and is not anonymized information.

When audio listings are retrieved and when a code is applied, the Operator’s server sends the customer ID to RevenueCat to check entitlement status and, when a code is applied, requests a time-limited Premium grant. Entitlement results are cached briefly by the delivery infrastructure. The ledger used to validate codes stores a matching value, code conditions and redemption counts, but not the codes themselves or customer IDs. The Operator and providers such as Cloudflare may keep access and operational records, including source IP addresses, for delivery and security purposes; their retention is described in Article 6.

The Service does not use personal data for recommendations or profiling. Word Shuffle uses random selection without personal data as input.

### 1.3 Information Received in Inquiries

Emails to `contact@toapps.dev` include the sender’s address, any name voluntarily supplied, message/attachments and information needed to match purchases. Delivery and storage use Cloudflare Email Routing and the destination email service. This information is used to respond to inquiries and rights requests and retain necessary handling records. Do not send diagnoses, detailed health information, passwords, full payment-card numbers or unnecessary identity documents.

### 1.4 Other Information

The app has no features collecting GPS location, microphone recordings, camera images, contacts, photo libraries or biometric measurements such as HealthKit data. There is no profile registration for names, addresses, dates of birth or email addresses. Inquiries and voluntary text input are addressed above.

No external services for analytics or crash reporting are used (the advertising SDK’s own measurement and diagnostics and the update-recovery communications described in Section 1.2 do occur). Push tokens are not obtained for local notifications. Session-interruption notifications may not be scheduled depending on age-signal results (Article 9).

## Article 2 Purposes of Use

Information is handled to provide features, save settings, deliver operational settings (minimum supported version, download suspension and notices), verify purchases/restores/delivery entitlements, validate codes and provide time-limited Premium, deliver updates, maintain security, handle inquiries and rights requests, manage consent, keep records of consent to automatic renewal, serve and measure non-personalized advertising, and meet legal obligations. New purposes will be subject to required notice, consent and other procedures.

## Article 3 Third Parties and Business Succession

Personal data is not disclosed to third parties without consent except where permitted or required by law. Necessary service providers and overseas handling are addressed in Articles 4 and 5. If information is taken over as part of a business succession, such as a business transfer or merger, the Operator will take the measures required by law and the information will be handled within the existing purposes of use.

## Article 4 External Providers

RevenueCat, Cloudflare, Expo, GitHub, Google LLC and its affiliates (Google Mobile Ads and UMP) and inquiry email delivery/storage providers are used for subscription management, delivery, keeping records of consent to automatic renewal, updates, public pages, non-personalized advertising delivery and measurement, ad-related consent management, and email handling. Information and purposes are listed in Article 1. This does not mean that all processing, including Google’s advertising services, is performed on the Operator’s behalf; roles depend on each service’s terms, settings and applicable law. Where handling is entrusted to service providers, it is limited to what is necessary and managed by reviewing contract terms and handling practices.

Apple and Google independently handle store accounts, payment and their own purchase records. Not all store activities are performed on the Operator’s behalf. OS cloud backups likewise depend on the User’s OS account and settings.

## Article 5 Overseas Handling

External providers include businesses based in the United States. Delivery infrastructure and CDNs, such as Cloudflare’s, process data in multiple countries and regions, including Japan; destinations are not limited to the United States. Google’s advertising services may also process information in multiple countries and regions, including outside the User’s country of residence. This Article also applies to RevenueCat’s processing and Cloudflare R2’s storage of records relating to consent to automatic renewal.

Where handling constitutes an international provision of personal data, applicable legal requirements include reviewing protections and relevant systems and providing information, obtaining consent or following other necessary procedures. Questions about countries and safeguards may be sent to the contact in Article 14.

## Article 6 Security and Retention

1. The Operator takes necessary and appropriate security measures for the personal data it handles, such as restricting access to personal data on the Operator’s side to what is necessary for its work, encrypting communications (HTTPS), managing accounts and credentials for external services, and reviewing the contracts and settings of service providers. Where data is handled in a foreign country, the Operator takes these measures with an understanding of that country’s personal-information protection system. Details of the measures, except those whose disclosure could interfere with security management, will be provided without undue delay upon request to the contact in Article 14. The Operator has no feature collecting or storing store passwords or full card numbers.
2. Information on the device is kept until the User deletes it or the app deletes records that are no longer needed. Entitlement caches in the delivery infrastructure expire after a short time but may be created again on subsequent use. Subscription, update, delivery and advertising records, and access and operational records kept by the Operator and providers, are retained according to each service’s settings, terms and applicable law. Records relating to consent to automatic renewal kept by the Operator for legal compliance are governed by paragraph 4.
3. Inquiry and rights-request records are kept as needed for handling and deleted when no longer required. Records may be retained to the extent needed for legal retention, disputes, fraud prevention or other legitimate requirements. Users may inquire about retention or deletion of specific records.
4. Records needed to verify consent to automatic renewal, and the order and transaction information needed to match that consent to a purchase, are retained for the period required by applicable law, including U.S. state law. This retention does not end because automatic renewal is turned off, the app is uninstalled, or the customer record in the subscription management service (RevenueCat) is deleted. Other purchase-action records are retained as long as needed for purchase processing, matching and related purposes. In either case, records that no longer need to be kept are deleted. Purchase-action records on the device are kept as long as needed to confirm transmission and allow resending, and are deleted the next time the app processes them after that period. If a purchase is canceled, the app deletes records not yet sent, but records already passed to the SDK may still be sent.

## Article 7 Access, Correction, Deletion and Other Requests

1. Requests for access, correction, deletion, cessation of use or other rights under applicable law may be sent to the email address in Article 14.
2. To help identify the relevant information, please describe the request and tell us the OS used, the purchasing store and similar details. Purchase-related requests may be matched using a support ID and only the necessary parts of purchase evidence. An ID alone is not treated as proof of identity. Passwords and full card numbers are not requested.
3. The Operator responds to requests without undue delay and within any deadline set by applicable law. If an extension or further investigation is needed, the reason will be explained.
4. “Reset to Defaults” in Settings resets settings such as theme and volume; it does not erase all information. Custom Word Sets and downloaded audio can be deleted from their respective management screens. Other information on the device (including the customer ID and similar data stored by SDKs, and the setting and records for reminders before a subscription renews) is erased by clearing the app’s data or uninstalling the app. Whether to receive reminders before a subscription renews can be changed on the in-app subscription management screen. However, restoring from an OS backup may bring back the same ID and similar data, and restoring purchases may link them to records again. Uninstalling the app does not erase OS backups, store purchase history or records held by providers or email services. Instructions will be provided for the relevant data.
5. After matching the request, the Operator will process deletion or cessation of use for records under its control, including relevant RevenueCat records. Requests concerning a store’s own purchase records will be directed to that store. Google’s advertising records follow its settings, terms, applicable law and procedures; the Operator cannot directly delete all such records. Reusing the app or restoring purchases may recreate records. Data deletion does not cancel a subscription; cancellation must be completed with the purchasing store. However, requests for deletion, cessation of use or other action are assessed under applicable law. Only to the extent retention is required, the records described in Article 6(4) are retained for the period specified there. After the RevenueCat customer record is deleted, necessary copies remain in private Cloudflare R2 storage controlled by the Operator. These copies are used only for legally required recordkeeping and verification of compliance. If any part of a request cannot be fulfilled, the Operator explains the reason, the scope of the information retained and the retention period, and processes deletion, cessation of use or other action for the remaining information under applicable law.

## Article 8 Cookies, Advertising and Analytics

The app uses an advertising SDK after the Terms and this Policy have been accepted to serve non-personalized ads. Non-personalized ads are not selected based on past behavior, but identifiers and connection information may still be processed for frequency capping, aggregate reporting and fraud prevention. The app does not use a separate analytics SDK. Subscription and update identifiers, and the information and purposes associated with the advertising SDK, are described in Articles 1, 2 and 4. External store or support pages follow their providers’ cookie policies.

The app does not respond to browser “Do Not Track” signals or similar mechanisms. The Operator itself does not collect information about Users’ online activities over time and across different websites or online services. However, even with non-personalized settings, Google Mobile Ads may process advertising identifiers and connection information over time for purposes such as frequency capping, aggregate reporting and fraud prevention, and may associate them with ad serving in other apps and websites.

## Article 9 Children and Minors

The Service is not directed to children under 13, and persons under 13 may not use it. If the Operator learns that personal information of a child under 13 has been collected, it will stop relevant processing, make necessary checks, delete information and take other steps required by applicable law. Parents may contact the address in Article 14. Minors must obtain the consent of a legal representative as required by law.

In the Android app, to comply with U.S. state laws, when the storefront country of the Google Play account is the United States (including when the storefront country cannot be determined), the app obtains age signals from Google Play (age range, the type of method by which that age information was established, age verification status, and the status of parental approval of significant changes to the app) after the User agrees to the Terms and this Policy. The app does not obtain age signals when the storefront country is known to be outside the United States. If the signals indicate that the User is under 13, that the User is aged 13 to 17 based solely on self-declaration (excluding age information obtained through parental supervision or age verification), that age verification is required, or that parental approval has not been given, the app displays a screen that stops use of the Service. Age signals are handled only in the device’s memory, are not stored, and are not sent to the Operator’s servers or to advertising or subscription management providers. Other information in the response from Google Play (the date of a significant change approved by a parent, and an identifier assigned to installations by supervised users) is processed on the device by the Google library built into the app; the app’s own code does not read, store or send it.

On launches that retrieve age signals, until the first result is available, and while the result indicates that the User is under 18, that age verification is required or that parental approval has not been given, or if retrieval fails, the app does not perform ad-related processing (consent checks, advertising SDK initialization, and ad requests and display) and does not schedule session-interruption notifications. If a later retrieval returns a result indicating none of these, ad-related processing starts from that point. However, the app cannot necessarily stop communications for measurement, diagnostics, security or similar purposes by an advertising SDK initialized earlier. Age-signal contents are never provided to advertising providers.

Users aged 13 to 17 and their parents or guardians may also make the requests described in Article 7. Age-signal information can be reviewed in Google Account settings. Questions or objections about the decision (including requests for re-review) are accepted at the contact in Article 14.

Even before an age signal is obtained, communications involving the customer ID and similar information take place after consent to initialize subscription management and determine the storefront country. Because age signals are not stored in association with the customer ID, age signals alone cannot identify the Operator’s records. When an inquiry or request is received, the Operator matches the relevant records using the minimum necessary information and responds under Article 7 and applicable law.

## Article 10 Incidents

If the Operator becomes aware of a data breach or other security incident, such as unauthorized disclosure, loss or damage of personal data, it will investigate the incident, contain it and take measures to prevent recurrence. The Operator will report to supervisory authorities and notify affected individuals as required by law, using appropriate methods such as public pages, notices accessible in the app and email to known contact addresses.

## Article 11 Revisions

Changes and their effective date will be announced at the public URL or through other appropriate channels. Legally required consent will be requested for material changes, such as changes in collected information or purposes. The app may also ask Users to accept revised documents.

## Article 12 Users in the United States and Canada

The Operator does not sell personal information for monetary or other valuable consideration and does not share it for cross-context behavioral advertising. Information is sent to Google LLC and its affiliates for non-personalized advertising (Section 1.2). Ad requests specify Google’s Restricted Data Processing, requesting that Google handle the information as a service provider and processor under U.S. state privacy laws, subject to Google’s terms. However, non-personalized settings do not necessarily mean that no processing falls within legal definitions of sale or sharing. Requests to exercise rights under the laws of the User’s place of residence are accepted through the contact in Article 7. The individual proprietor is responsible for personal information protection.
